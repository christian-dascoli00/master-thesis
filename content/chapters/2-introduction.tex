\chapter{The categorical structure of Rings}

\fontsize{12}{14.5}\selectfont

In this chapter, we aim to introduce a motivating example for the notions of ideally exact categories. This is based on an article by George Janelidze \cite{Janelidze2024}.

Consider the category $\mathbf{Ring}$ of rings with unit. The notion of ideal is, at first glance, somewhat unnatural in the context of categories. In fact, in general, an ideal of a ring with unit is not an object in the category $\mathbf{Ring}$: if an ideal contains the unit, then it coincides with the whole ring. However, we observe that it is an object of the category $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ of rings without unit.

It is a standard fact that $\mathbf{Ring}$ and $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ are finitely complete, protomodular and exact categories. They both have binary coproducts, even if they are not the same. The coproduct in the category $\mathbf{Ring}$ is the free product, while in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ it is the free product with amalgamation, in order to ensure that there is a unit in the coproduct.

That said, they have a crucial difference: $\mathbf{Ring}$ is not pointed, whereas $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ is. In fact, in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ the initial and terminal objects are both the zero ring ${0}$, while in $\mathbf{Ring}$ the initial object is the zero ring $0=\mathbb{Z}$ of integers, but the terminal object is the ring $1={0}$. Thus, we conclude the $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ is semiabelian, while $\mathbf{Ring}$ fails to be.

So we have the forgetful functor that forgets the unit
\[U: \mathbf{Ring} \to \mathbf{Ring_*}\]
with $\mathbf{Ring}$ protomodular and exact, and $\mathbf{Ring_*}$. This functor has a left adjoint: the unitalization. This functor, also known as the Dorroh extension, is given by
\begin{align*}
F: \mathbf{Ring_*} &\to \mathbf{Ring} \\
R &\mapsto \mathbb{Z} \oplus R
\end{align*}
where the sum $\mathbb{Z} \oplus R$ is the sum by components, while the product $\cdot$ is defined as follows
\begin{align*}
\cdot : (\mathbb{Z} \oplus R) \times (\mathbb{Z} \oplus R) &\to \mathbb{Z} \oplus R \\
((m,r),(n,s))&\mapsto (mn, rs + ms + nr)
\end{align*}
and with unit of $(\mathbb{Z} \oplus R)$ given by $(1,0)$.

The unit and the counit of the adjunction are given by
\begin{align*}
\eta_R: R &\to UF(R)=\mathbb{Z} \oplus R \\
r &\mapsto (0,r)
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
\epsilon_R: FU(R)=\mathbb{Z} \oplus R &\to R  \\
(n,r) &\mapsto (n1_R + r)
\end{align*}

The functor $U$ turns out to be monadic, simply by Beck's theorem, since coequalizers in $\mathbf{Ring}$ and $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ are the same. In fact, given $f,g$ morphisms, the coequalizer of $(f,g)$ is the canonical projection
\[\pi: B \to B/I \ \ \ \ \ \ \ I = (f(r) - g(r) \ | \ r \in R)\]
This adjunction will lead us to the definition of the ideally exact categories. We will see that the definition involves a  generic monadic functor in a semiabelian category. Sometimes, as we will see, such functor is, up to category equivalences, the pullback functor of $!:0 \to 1$. That's the case for the functor $U$ above. As we will see, this is intrinsically due to the fact that the unit $\eta$ of the adjunction is cartesian. In fact, one can easily prove, for each $R$,$R'$ in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ and for each morphism $f:R \to R'$, that the pullback in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ of $\eta_{R'}$ and $f$
\begin{align*}
R'&\times_{UF(R')} UF(R) = R'\times_{\mathbb{Z}\oplus R'} (\mathbb{Z}\oplus R) = \\[1.5ex]
&= \{ (r',(n,r)) \in R'\times (\mathbb{Z}\oplus R) \ | \  UF(f)(x) = \eta_{R'}(y) \} \\[1.5ex]
&=\{ (r',(n,r)) \in R'\times (\mathbb{Z}\oplus R) \ | \ n=0 \ \land \ f(r)=r' \} \\[1.5ex]
&=\{ (f(r),(0,r)) \ | \  r \in R \}
\end{align*}
is isomorphic to $R$.

We now want to deep into the mentioned category equivalence. First we define the pullback functor
\[!^*:(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 1) \to (\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 0)\]
Let $R$ be an object in $\mathbf{Ring}$, then $!^*(R)=(0 \times_1 R,  \mathrm{proj}_1)$
\begin{equation}\label{ring square}

\begin{tikzcd}
0\times_1 R \arrow[rr, "\mathrm{proj}_2"] \arrow[d, "\mathrm{proj}_1"] &  & R \arrow[d, "!^R"] \\
0 \arrow[rr, "!"]                                                      &  & 1                 
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

Observe that
\[(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 1) \cong \mathbf{Ring}\]
in fact, we can always provide an object in $\mathbf{Ring}$ with a morphism to $1$, just using the unique morphism to $1$. Furthermore
\[(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 0) = (\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbf{Ring_*} \]
where the equivalence of category is given by the unitalization functor 
\begin{align*}
    (F,\pi_\mathbb{Z}) : \mathbf{Ring_*} &\to \mathbf{Ring} \downarrow \mathbb{Z}\\
    R &\mapsto (\mathbb{Z} \oplus R, \pi_\mathbb{Z}:\mathbb{Z} \oplus R \to \mathbb{Z})
\end{align*}
and its quasi inverse $\mathrm{Ker}$\footnote{We are using the algebraic definition of $\mathrm{Ker}$, in fact we cannot use the categorical definition since $\mathbf{Ring}$ is not pointed. However, one can still induce an action of $\mathrm{Ker}$ in the morphisms. Alternatively, one may consider the categorical definition of $\mathrm{Ker}$, but it is the $\mathrm{Ker}$ in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$, which is pointed, via the forgetful functor $U$. This perspective will be used when we treat the corresponding theorem in an ideally exact category, where the existence of the associated categorical equivalence is established.}.
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{Ker} : (\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow \mathbb{Z}) &\to \mathbf{Ring_*}\\
    (R,\phi:R \to \mathbb{Z}) &\mapsto \mathrm{Ker}(\phi) 
\end{align*}

The following diagram, which is commutative up to natural isomorphism, summarizes the situation and shows that the forgetful functor $U$ is, up to equivalence of categories, the pullback functor $!^*$.

Observe that the downward-right directed square commutes up to natural isomorphism, since, referring to the diagram \refeq{ring square}, given an object $R$ in $\mathbf{Ring}$, $\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{proj}_1)$ is isomorphic to $R$ in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$.
On the other hand, the upward-left directed square also commutes up to natural isomorphism, because in both cases we applied the unitalization functor $F$.

As already noticed, ideals in $\mathbf{Ring}$ are not objects in $\mathbf{Ring}$. However they are the normal subobject, ie the kernel of an arrow, in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$, which is semiabelian. Our aim is to study the notion of ideally exact category, that better captures the categorical structure of rings and ideals.

Remark that this anomaly does not occur in the category $\mathbf{Grp}$. In fact normal subgroups are objects in the category of groups and, since $\mathbf{Grp}$ is semiabelian, they are exactly the normal subobjects in the semiabelian sense.
