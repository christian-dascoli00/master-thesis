\chapter{The categorial structure of Rings}

\fontsize{12}{14.5}\selectfont

In this chapter, we aim to introduce a motivating example for the notions of ideally exact categories. 

Consider the category $\mathbf{Ring}$ of rings with unity. The notion of ideal is, at first glance, an somewhat unnatural in the context of categories. In fact, in general, an ideal of a ring with unit is not an object in the category $\mathbf{Ring}$: if an ideal contains the unit, then it coincides with the whole ring. However, we observe that it is an object of the category $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ of rings without unit.

It is a standard fact that $\mathbf{Ring}$ and $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ are finitely complete, protomodular and exact categories. They both have binary coproducts, even if they are not the same. The coproduct in the category $\mathbf{Ring}$ is the free product, while in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ it is the free product with amalgamation, in order to ensure that there is a unit in the coproduct.

That said, they have a crucial difference: $\mathbf{Ring}$ is not pointed, whereas $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ is. In fact, in $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ the initial and terminal objects are both the zero ring ${0}$, while in $\mathbf{Ring}$ the initial object is the zero ring $0=\mathbb{Z}$ of integers, but the terminal object is the ring $1={0}$. Thus, we conclude the $\mathbf{Ring_*}$ is semiabelian, while $\mathbf{Ring}$ fails to be.

In particular we have the forgetful functor that forgets the unit.
\[U: \mathbf{Ring} \to \mathbf{Ring_*}\]
with $\mathbf{Ring}$ protomodular and exact, and $\mathbf{Ring_*}$. This functor turns out to be monadic, with left adjoint the unitalization functor.
\begin{align*}
F: \mathbf{Ring_*} &\to \mathbf{Ring} \\
R &\mapsto \mathbb{Z} \oplus R
\end{align*}
This adjunction will lead us to the definition of the ideally exact categories. 

Moreover, the functor $U$ is, up to category equivalences, the pullback functor $!^*$ where $!$ is the unique morphism in $\mathbf{Ring}$ $!:0 \to 1$ and 
\[!^*:(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 1) \to (\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 0)\]
is defined as follows. Let $R$ be an object in $\mathbf{Ring}$, then $!^*(X)=(0 \times_1 R,  \mathrm{proj}_1)$

Let's find out the mentioned equivalences of categories. Observe that
\[(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 1) \cong \mathbf{Ring}\]
in fact, we can always provide an object in $\mathbf{Ring}$ with a morphism to $1$, just using the unique morphism to $1$. Furthermore
\[(\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow 0) = (\mathbf{Ring} \downarrow \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbf{Ring_*} \]
where the equivalence of category is given by the unitalization functor $(F,\pi)$, and its quasi inverse $\mathrm{ker}$.
