\begin{Abstract}
    \begin{changemargin}{1cm}{1cm}
    \fontsize{12}{14.5}\selectfont
    This thesis investigates the notion of \textit{ideally exact category}, a non-pointed
    counterpart of semiabelian categories. Rather than merely dropping the
    pointedness assumption, the aim is to retain as much as possible of the
    categorical structure inherent to semiabelian categories. The theory is motivated and illustrated by a guiding example: unital rings. Indeed, ideals
    of a unital ring may at first appear categorically unnatural, since they are
    themselves non-unital rings. This new perspective allows for a deeper understanding of ideals, and highlights how category theory can help clarify
    and generalize classical algebraic notions.
    \end{changemargin}
\end{Abstract}
