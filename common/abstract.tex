\begin{Abstract}
    \begin{changemargin}{1cm}{1cm}
    \fontsize{12}{14.5}\selectfont
    This thesis investigates non-pointed counterpart of the notion of semiabelian category. Rather than simply dropping pointedness, we aim to keep some of the categorical structure inherent to semiabelian categories. This leads us to the definition of ideally exact categories. This notion captures the categorical structure of unital rings. Indeed, at first glance, ideals of a unital ring seem somewhat unnatural in a categorical sense, since they are not unital rings. This perspective allows for a deeper understanding of ideals and highlights how categorical tools can clarify and generalize classical algebraic notions. This study employs several categorical tools, such as the pullback functor, lifting properties in the Eilenberg-Moore category and effective descent morphisms.
    \end{changemargin}
\end{Abstract}
